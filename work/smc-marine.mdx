## The problem

SMC Marine runs mobile plant safety and asset management as a single operation — spanning
civil equipment alongside boats and barges. The systems available assumed a uniform
operation, one kind of asset and one kind of site, and none of them fit an operation built
around several at once.

## The choice

SafetyCulture was selected because it worked well in the field and had a well-documented open
API — confidence it could be extended rather than merely used. At the time, its inspection
scheduling and analytics were weak.

## What was built

1. A multitenant sync of SafetyCulture data into Postgres, connected to Tableau for dashboards
   and to drive service scheduling from SafetyCulture actions.
2. Once the data was in Postgres, the surface opened up: telemetry came in directly from
   machines — Hitachi, Soilmec — alongside aftermarket Digital Matter GPS trackers, feeding GPS
   locations geofenced into descriptions back into SafetyCulture.
3. Hour meter readings and odometer estimates from the trackers feed SafetyCulture's sensor
   module, so service intervals are live rather than estimated.
4. An AI email service reads every comment, task and timeline item and summarises them into a
   management email at 6am Monday.

## The shape of it

SafetyCulture is the field capture layer. Data comes out to be enriched and returns as
something the system couldn't produce alone. The database is timeseries and GIS, and pulls in
more than SafetyCulture — IoT GPS feeds, HR and finance systems included.

It's headless, so it earns its keep the moment it's stood up — no portal, no logins, nothing
new for a team to learn. Built for an operation the size of SMC Marine's, not an enterprise IT
estate. Every client's mix of assets has turned out different enough that packaging this as one
product never made sense — it stayed a service, shaped around each operation, on purpose.
