**SMC Marine** · Durable physical asset tagging — civil & marine equipment · Originated within the SMC Marine build, spun out as QRtub in 2025

## The problem

SMC Marine needed durable asset tagging across a fleet mixing civil equipment with boats and
barges — physical labels that would survive years in the field, on assets from small tools to
multi-tonne plant. The practical constraint mattered as much as the technical one: plates get
cut as a sheet, and labels come in runs, so whatever scheme was used had to fit how tags are
actually produced and applied, not just how they're read.

## What was built

A QR-based tagging system, built as an in-house tool for SMC Marine specifically. The core
idea: treat the code as infrastructure, not a destination. Instead of baking a URL into a
sticker and hoping nothing changes, the physical code was decoupled from what it resolves to —
so a link keeps working through system migrations, re-pointing, and multi-system routing,
without reprinting a single tag. One code could carry every option a scanner might need — an
operator, a customer, a maintenance contractor could all scan the same tag and pick what they
came for, rather than the tag trying to guess who was holding the phone.

## The generalization

It turned out to be the one piece of Teralis's bespoke work that generalized beyond a single
client. What started as SMC Marine's in-house solution became QRtub — now an independent
product, run and sold separately at [qrtub.com](https://qrtub.com). Codes have been in the wild
since 2025, on assets from 5kg to 500t: cranes, boats, barges, surf lifesaving stations, piling
hammers, trucks.

This page is about where QRtub came from, not what it does today — qrtub.com is the source of
truth for that. Everything else on this site is Teralis's bespoke work; QRtub is the one
exception that stopped being bespoke.
